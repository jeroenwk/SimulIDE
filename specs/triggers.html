Circuit triggers

A trigger watches the running simulation and acts when its condition is met, the way a scope's trigger does: pause at the step it happened, save a key frame there (or a moment before), or count. Triggers are set on a part from its properties window, or in the circuit's Triggers tab, and saved with the circuit.

From the user, 2026-11-22:

  2. Each row names what it watches: a part label and pin number, or a node, or several of them when more are involved, and the trigger values in volts and/or amperes.
  3. Triggers on a part are set directly from its properties window, in a Triggers tab of that window.
  4. The scope: its properties window gets a Triggers tab showing its trigger channel and level (Auto or a voltage), editable there (it is the scope's own setting); a checkbox makes it a simulation trigger, which adds a row to the circuit's triggers; deleting that row only clears the checkbox (D21).

2. What a trigger is

A trigger watches one signal with one condition, and has an action and a mode. Triggers are combined with AND in the circuit's Triggers tab (§3, Combining rows): the combined row has the action and mode, its members are its conditions. The signals:

SignalWritten in a rowNotes
A part's state, or one block of itIC1 supply (state)Scripted cores (component.setStatus) and the built-in parts with a StateLog (BJT, MOSFET, SCR, triac, diac, op amp, regulator, diode, zener, LED, 555, relay, analog mux)
A pin's voltage to groundIC1 pin 3 VDDPin number and name; two-pin parts: pin 1, pin 2
The voltage between two pinsQ1 pins G–S (Vgs)A familiar name where there is one (Vgs, Vds, Vbe, Vce); the pins may be of two parts: R5 pin 2 – C3 pin 1 (then shown in both parts' tabs)

A subcircuit (IC1 is one) is watched at its package pins and through the state blocks its cores report; its inner nodes are not offered in the first version. Conditions in the first version:

KindOnConditionShown as
Statestateany change · from → to (either may be "any") · reaches a severity (ON, WAIT, FAULT)ON → UVLO, any → RUN, any change, ≥ FAULT
EdgeV or Icrosses a level: rising, falling or either; hysteresis↑ 4.0 V, ↓ 8.0 V, ↕ 0 A
WindowV or Ileaves or enters a band (low … high); hysteresisoutside 0 … 1.2 A, inside 4.5 … 5.5 V

Events and qualifiers. An AND has one member that happens (the event, ⚡: it gives the time) and members that must be true at that moment (qualifiers). A member used as a qualifier is read as the state its condition leads to:

Member's conditionAs a qualifier
↑ Lis above L (true from L up, false once below L − hysteresis)
↓ Lis below L (the mirror)
outside a … b / inside a … bis outside / is inside
A → B, any → B, ≥ FAULTis B / is at least FAULT
↕ L, any changeno state: such a member can only be the event

So an AND has exactly one event; it may hold at most one event-only member (↕, any change), which then is the event (Combine with AND is greyed out with the reason otherwise). A combined trigger always has a time.

Why no OR. Separate rows already act as an OR: each fires on its own signal. An OR row would only add shared settings (one single, one holdoff, one count for the group) and one hit when two events fall on the same step. That is left for later, with nesting (§14, D24).

Managing rows

  • Select: click; Shift for a range, ⌘ to add one. Arm / disarm: the row's box (with several selected, a click on one box sets them all) or the context menu. Space stays play / pause, as in Key frames and on the canvas.
  • Edit: double-click or Enter opens the trigger editor (§4, the same form as in a part's properties) as a small dialog; for a trigger of one part, "Show in part" opens that part's properties at its Triggers tab.
  • Delete: Backspace or the context menu, after a question ("Delete these 2 triggers?"). Undoable (Edit → Undo), as triggers are part of the circuit (D18). The scope's row only clears its checkbox (D21).
  • New: + opens the editor with "Watch" empty: pick a part and its pin, pin pair or state from lists, or a node by its net name (picking on the canvas: later). A part's own triggers are usually made in its properties (§4), a node's from a wire's context menu (§5).
  • While running: triggers can be added, edited, armed and deleted during a run; the change applies at the next frame (worker idle) and does not end the history of the run.
  • Select on the canvas: selecting rows highlights their parts and pins (as Messages' part lines do).

Combining rows: AND

An AND is made here, from rows that already exist, whatever parts or nodes they watch:

  1. Select two or more rows (Shift / ⌘) and choose Combine with AND (⌘G).
  2. One combined row replaces them, with the members indented under it (▾ folds them). The combined row takes the action and mode of the first selected row. The members keep their own settings, unused while combined (shown greyed in the part's tab), so Split gives back the rows as they were.
  3. For an AND, the event (⚡) is the first selected member that is an edge, a window crossing or a state transition; a click on the ⚡ column of another member makes it the event. The others are qualifiers, read as states at the event's step (§2).
  4. Split (context menu of the combined row, ⇧⌘G) puts the members back as independent rows with their own settings, disarmed.
  5. A member's watch and condition are edited like any row (double-click). Adding a row to a combination: select the combination and the row, Combine with AND: they merge. Removing one: Split, then combine the others again. No nesting and no dragging in the first version (§14).
  6. Deleting a part removes its members from combinations; a combination left with one member becomes that row (with the combination's action and mode), with none it is deleted.

4. The Triggers tab of a part's properties

Every part whose pins or state can be watched gets a Triggers tab in its properties window (the dialog of a double-click), after its property groups and before Keyboard. It has two parts:

  • The list: the triggers on this part (the same rows as the circuit's tab, with "Watches" written without the part's label). Armed boxes, multi-select, + and −.
  • The editor: the selected row, or a new one after +. Changes apply at once (as the other property tabs do) and are one undo step per change.

The editor's "Watch" offers only what the part has: its states (if it reports any), its pins by number and name, pin pairs, pin currents. Triggers are single-signal here; AND is made in the circuit's tab (§3, Combining rows, D22). A trigger that is a member of a combined row shows an in AND tag; the combination's action and mode apply (its own are kept, greyed, for a Split), with a link "Edit the combination…".

5. Triggers on a node

A node that belongs to no single part (an output rail, a net between several parts) is watched from:

  • the + of the circuit's Triggers tab: "Watch: node", then a click on a wire on the canvas, or a tunnel / net name from a list;
  • a wire's context menu: Trigger on this node…, which opens the editor with that node.

The row shows the net's name when it has one (a tunnel name, a label), else node n12 with the parts it connects in the tooltip (R5 pin 2, C3 pin 1, IC1 pin 4 CS). The trigger is saved with one pin of the node as its anchor, so it survives renumbering. If the anchor goes (its wire or its part deleted), another pin of the same net takes over; with none left the trigger is disarmed with a line in Messages. A node trigger is not deleted with a part (unlike a trigger on that part's own pins or state, D18).

6. Firing: exact rules

  • When: conditions are evaluated on solved steps only (the step converged, as part states are reported). An edge fires on the first solved step at or past the level; its time is that step's time (no interpolation inside a step, unlike the scope's display, which interpolates). For a pause this is exact: the simulation stops on that step.
  • Hysteresis: a rising edge is armed below level − hysteresis and fires at level; falling the other way round; "either" arms on both sides. Default: 1 % of the level, at least 1 mV / 1 µA. A window uses the hysteresis inward on both edges.
  • Start of a run: the first solved step only sets the armed side (a signal that starts above a rising level does not fire until it has been below it).
  • State: fires at the state report's time (StateLog / setStatus times are exact). The states parts report at the first step of a run (their start state) set the starting point and do not fire.
  • AND (a combined row): on the event member's firing, the qualifier members are read at that same step, as states (§2). Members' own hysteresis applies to their events; a qualifier "is above L" uses the member's armed side (above L, below L − hysteresis is false).
  • Current into a pin: the pin's solved current; a two-pin part's current: its own current (positive from pin 1 to pin 2).

7. Actions and modes

The action is two checkboxes, Pause and Key frame (with "before Δ"); every trigger counts its hits whatever they are, so neither box ticked is "count only".

ActionWhat happens
PauseThe worker stops after the step that fired and the simulation pauses there; Messages: Trigger: IC1 supply ON → UVLO at 381.93 ms (paused). The row is selected in the Triggers tab.
Key frameA key frame is saved at that step, described by the trigger (IC1 supply: ON → UVLO, IC1 pin 3 VDD ↓ 8.0 V, or the combination's name). Needs a circuit saved to a file and parts that support it (§5.1 of sim-control.md); otherwise the row shows a warning icon with the reason, and on a hit the simulation pauses instead, with that reason in Messages. Triggers themselves work in every circuit, MCU circuits included.
Key frame, before ΔPre-trigger: the state at the trigger is kept (as a key frame's bytes, in memory), the run goes back Δ with the history (restore the keyframe before, replay without firing triggers), saves the key frame there, and loads the kept state again, so it carries on from the trigger with no second replay. Δ is limited by the history's span; with the history off, Δ is greyed out.
(neither)Count only: Hits and Last; trigger hits lists the times. No line per hit in Messages, a summary at pause and stop.
ModeWhat happens
singleFires once, then disarms itself (its box clears). Re-arm by the box.
normalFires each time. Holdoff: after a hit, ignore events for this long. Max: disarm after this many hits (empty: no limit).

Several triggers firing on the same step: all their actions run (key frames in row order), one pause.

8. With skips, going back and range play

  • Skip forward (→, ⏭, sim skip): triggers stay active; a pause trigger ends the skip at its step (like a breakpoint), and the skip reports it ("Skip: stopped by trigger IC1 supply at 381.93 ms").
  • Going back (←, ⏮, a key frame click): the replay that brings the circuit to the target time does not fire triggers; hits after the target time are removed (Hits and Last follow the history).
  • Range play of key frames: triggers are active; a pause trigger ends the range early.
  • sim run --for: a pause trigger ends it early; the reply says which trigger and when.
  • Opening a key frame: hits after its time are removed; the triggers' running state (which side each is armed on, holdoff, single already fired) comes from the key frame where it holds that trigger, else from the signal's value at that time (§10).
  • Key frames saved by triggers appear in the Key frames tab like any other, with the trigger's description.

9. Saved in the .sim1 (D18)

A <triggers> element after the items, one <trigger> per row. Signals refer to parts by their uid (CircId), so renaming a label changes nothing; pins by their id. Deleting a part deletes the triggers that watch it, in the same undo step.

<triggers>
  <trigger id="3" armed="1" pause="0" keyframe="1" before="1e-3" mode="single">
    <cond sig="volt" comp="MT7833A-1" pin="VDD" ref="gnd" edge="fall" level="8" hyst="0.1"/>
  </trigger>
  <trigger id="4" armed="1" pause="1" mode="single" join="and" name="current sense while running">
    <trigger id="7" event="1" pause="1" mode="single"><cond sig="volt" comp="MT7833A-1" pin="CS" edge="rise" level="0.5"/></trigger>
    <trigger id="8"><cond sig="state" comp="MT7833A-1" block="pwm" from="any" to="RUN"/></trigger>
  </trigger>
  <trigger id="5" armed="1" mode="normal">
    <cond sig="volt" comp="Mosfet-7" pin="G" ref="S" edge="rise" level="4" hyst="0.1"/>
  </trigger>
  <trigger id="6" armed="1" keyframe="1" mode="normal" holdoff="1e-3">
    <cond sig="volt" node="Resistor-5:rPin" window="out" low="30" high="36"/>
  </trigger>
</triggers>

A combined row is a <trigger join="and"> (the attribute leaves room for OR later) holding its member triggers, each with its id and its own settings (unused while combined, restored by Split); event="1" marks the AND's event. Copy / paste of a part copies its own single-signal triggers with it (new ids, same armed state); combinations are not copied.

The scope's simulation trigger is an ordinary row with <cond sig="scope" comp="Oscope-1"/>: its channel and level are read from the scope's own Trigger / TrigAuto / TrigLevel, so they are never stored twice. Hits are run results and are not saved.

10. Engine

  • State conditions hook the existing reports (StateLog::report, ScriptCpu::setStatus), which already run at the solved step with exact times: no cost when no trigger watches that part.
  • Voltage and current conditions are checked after each solved step, on the watched signals only: at the start of a run the triggers are compiled into a flat list of small checks (node pointer or pin pointer, level, hysteresis, armed side). A circuit without triggers pays one empty-list test per step.
  • A pause sets the worker's stop at the current time (the mechanism of sim run --for and skips), so the step is complete and a keyframe can be taken there.
  • Keyframes: the triggers' running state (armed side, holdoff end, hits, single fired) goes in its own section after the part states, keyed by trigger id and tolerant: a trigger added since is derived from its signal at that time, one deleted since is skipped. So adding or editing triggers never makes a saved key frame refuse to load. The checks are not elements: the layout signature does not change, and the electrical hash of a key frame ignores the <triggers> element (no "the circuit changed" warning for a trigger edit).
  • Measured on board_mains before merging: speed with 0, 5 and 20 voltage triggers (expected: within the noise for 5).

12. Tests

tests/regress/test_simctl_triggers.py, with the expected times from the circuits, not from the simulator:

  • RC charge (10 V, 1 kΩ, 1 µF): a rising trigger at 6.32 V pauses at τ = 1 ms (within one step); falling and either on a discharge; a window on a square wave counts 2 crossings per period.
  • Hysteresis: a noisy signal around the level fires once per crossing, not per ripple.
  • State: board_mains (frozen copy), IC1 supply any → ON pauses at the same time as the state log reports it.
  • Combine: AND of a CS edge with pwm → RUN as qualifier fires only on edges during RUN; changing the event member; Split gives back the members; save and reopen keep the combination.
  • Pin pair: Vgs of a MOSFET driven through a resistor.
  • Actions: key frame (in the Key frames list, its description), key frame −1 ms (time = trigger − 1 ms, state identical to the one passed through), count (hits and times), single disarms, holdoff and max.
  • Run control: a skip stopped by a trigger; going back removes later hits and does not fire on the replay; replaying forward gives the same hits.
  • Saved: save, reopen, the triggers are the same; deleting a part deletes its triggers and undo brings them back, a node trigger moves its anchor; the scope's checkbox and its row.
  • Key frames and trigger edits: a key frame saved before a trigger is added, edited or deleted still loads, without a "circuit changed" warning, and the triggers' state after it is the one derived from the signals.
  • Start: a state "any change" trigger does not fire on the start states.

  • Measured values (mean / RMS over a window), settled (dV/dt below ε for T), power above a rating.
  • Sequences (A then B within T), the Nth occurrence; OR together with nested combinations ("VDD ↓ 8 V AND (pwm → RUN OR pwm → SOFT START)"), D24; dragging rows into and out of combinations; picking a pin or node on the canvas from the editor.
  • Capacitor charge-log events and other ratings as part states, so they can be state triggers.
  • Logic patterns; MCU: PC, register, RAM, UART bytes.
  • The scope redesign: dragging the level on the screen, slopes, Normal / Single on the scope, any trigger as the scope's source (the bench scope's EXT input).

Simpler UI without losing a feature:

  • Action as two checkboxes (Pause, Key frame with before Δ) instead of a list of four actions plus an "and" box: every trigger counts its hits anyway, so "count" is simply neither box.
  • The scope's simulation trigger is an ordinary row linked to the scope, instead of a special scope property with its own action and mode: one place for actions, and it can be combined like any row.
  • No nesting, no dragging of combinations in the first version: merge by combining a combination with rows, remove by Split and combine again. The same for one level of AND.
  • Pre-trigger without a second replay: the state at the trigger is kept and loaded back after the key frame is saved, instead of replaying forward again.
  • No canvas picking in the first version: lists in the editor, a wire's context menu for nodes.

Considered and kept: separate single / normal modes (single is normal with max 1, but they are the bench scope's words); a dialog editor for the circuit's tab (the bottom pane is too short for an editor under the list).
